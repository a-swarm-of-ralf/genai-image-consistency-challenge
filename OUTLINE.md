# One Nice Image Is Easy

> **Generating one great image is easy. Now make six.**

Welcome to the **GenAI Image Consistency Challenge**.

In this workshop, you'll go beyond generating a single impressive image. Your team will create a short visual story with recurring characters, objects, locations, and a consistent visual style.

Then we'll see how well it survives review — and change.

## 🎯 The Challenge

Your team will create a visual story consisting of **5–8 images**.

Your story should:

- Tell a recognizable story from beginning to end
- Use the same protagonist(s) throughout
- Maintain a consistent visual style
- Keep recurring objects recognizable
- Maintain continuity between scenes
- Be something you would be comfortable publishing

You may use the available GenAI image-generation tools and their reference-image or editing capabilities.

**No manual image editing.**

---

# Challenge 1 — Tell a Story

## Exercise 1.1 — Choose Your Story

Choose a simple story that can be told in **5–8 scenes**.

Keep it simple. The challenge is not writing an elaborate story.

Good stories contain:

- One or two protagonists
- A recognizable location
- One or two recurring objects
- A clear beginning, middle, and end

### Example

**The Lost Key**

1. Mila leaves home carrying an old brass key.
2. She waits for a tram in the rain.
3. On the tram, she discovers the key is missing.
4. She searches for it.
5. Another passenger finds it beneath a seat.
6. Mila arrives at an old green door and unlocks it.

### Your story

Write down your **5–8 scenes** before generating any final images.

---

## Exercise 1.2 — Create Your Images

Now create an image for every scene.

How you approach this is up to you.

You can use:

- Prompts
- Reference images
- Image editing
- Previous generations
- Features provided by your chosen image model

Keep track of techniques that work particularly well — or particularly badly.

### Deliverable

A complete **5–8 image visual story**.

---

# Challenge 2 — Does It Actually Work?

Put all your images **next to each other**.

Don't evaluate them individually. Look at them as a single publication.

## Exercise 2.1 — Continuity Check

Look closely at your story.

### Characters

- Does the protagonist look like the same person?
- Is their age consistent?
- Hair?
- Face?
- Clothing?
- Accessories?
- Body proportions?

### Objects

- Are recurring objects actually the same objects?
- Are their shapes and proportions consistent?
- Do details appear or disappear?
- Do colors change?

### Visual Style

- Does every image appear to belong to the same publication?
- Is the rendering style consistent?
- Is the color palette consistent?
- Does the level of detail change?
- Does the visual mood remain appropriate?

### Story

- Can someone understand what happens without reading your prompts?
- Do consecutive scenes make visual sense?
- Are locations recognizable between images?

Write down the problems you find.

# Challenge 3 — Production Mode

You've made a story.

Now approach it as a **visual production**.

## Exercise 3.1 — Create a Style Bible

Define the visual rules for your story.

Consider:

- Illustration technique
- Shapes and linework
- Color palette
- Lighting
- Mood
- Level of detail
- Composition
- Camera language

### Example

> Warm modern children's-book illustration with soft rounded shapes, subtle watercolor texture, a muted orange-and-blue palette, European urban environments, natural lighting, and a friendly optimistic atmosphere.

Create one or more **style reference images** if your tool supports them.

---

## Exercise 3.2 — Create a Character Reference

Define your protagonist precisely.

Consider:

- Age
- Face
- Hair
- Body type
- Clothing
- Shoes
- Accessories
- Distinctive features

Create a useful character reference.

Depending on your tool, you might generate:

- Neutral pose
- Front view
- Side view
- Expressions
- Different poses

The objective isn't to create the prettiest character sheet.

The objective is to create a reference that helps you reproduce **the same character**.

---

## Exercise 3.3 — Don't Forget the Objects

Choose an important recurring object from your story.

Define what it actually looks like.

Create a reference image if useful.

It could be:

- A bicycle
- A backpack
- A product
- A vehicle
- A phone
- A piece of jewelry
- A mysterious brass key

---

## Exercise 3.4 — Try Again

Choose the **three weakest images** from your original story.

Regenerate or edit them using your new:

- Style definition
- Style references
- Character references
- Object references
- More structured scene descriptions

Compare the new images with the originals.

### Discuss

What improved?

What didn't?

What required more effort than expected?

Did solving one problem introduce another?

# Challenge 4 — The Client Has Feedback

🎉 Congratulations!

Your visual story has been reviewed.

Unfortunately, there are some changes.

## Exercise 4.1 — Global Change

Your facilitator will give you a change that must apply across the story.

For example:

> The protagonist's orange raincoat needs to be dark blue.

Update the affected images while preserving everything else.

---

## Exercise 4.2 — New Continuity Requirement

A new recurring element must be introduced.

For example:

> The protagonist must carry the same red backpack throughout the story.

Add it wherever appropriate.

Keep everything else consistent.

---

## Exercise 4.3 — Just One Tiny Change…

One image is almost perfect.

The client wants **one small change**.

For example:

> Keep scene 4 exactly as it is, but make the protagonist look at the bicycle instead of the camera.

Try to make the requested change while preserving everything that has already been approved.

### Discuss

What else changed?

Did you notice immediately?

Could you reliably reproduce the previous version?

---

# Challenge 5 — Quality Assurance

Your images are finished.

Or are they?

Exchange your story with another team.

**You are now responsible for deciding whether their images are ready for publication.**

## Exercise 5.1 — First Impression

Look at the complete story normally.

Ask yourself:

> **Would I approve this for publication?**

Make your decision before doing a detailed inspection.

---

## Exercise 5.2 — Inspect the Details

Now look carefully.

Zoom in where necessary.

Check:

- Hands and fingers
- Faces and eyes
- Clothing details
- Jewelry and accessories
- Text
- Logos
- Repeating patterns
- Architecture
- Furniture
- Reflections
- Shadows
- Background people
- Object intersections
- Perspective
- Recurring objects

Look for things that are **almost right**.

---

## Exercise 5.3 — Consistency Review

Compare images directly.

Review:

| Area | Question |
|---|---|
| Character | Is this convincingly the same person? |
| Clothing | Are details preserved? |
| Objects | Are recurring objects actually identical? |
| Environment | Do recurring locations make sense? |
| Style | Do all images belong to the same visual language? |
| Story | Does the sequence communicate what happened? |
| Details | Are there artifacts that would block publication? |
| Instructions | Does the output actually match the brief? |

Give the story back to its creators.

Compare what **they noticed** with what **you noticed**.

# Challenge 6 — Make It a Campaign

Your image has been approved!

Marketing now needs it in more places.

Naturally.

## Exercise 6.1 — Adapt an Approved Image

Choose one of your best images.

Create versions suitable for:

- **16:9** — website hero
- **1:1** — social media
- **9:16** — story / mobile

The requirements remain the same:

- Same character
- Same clothing
- Same objects
- Same style
- Same scene
- Same important details

But the composition should actually work in each format.

Where appropriate, leave usable space for copy.

**Don't just crop the original.**

# 🏁 The Final Question

At the beginning, the challenge was:

> **Can GenAI create a nice image?**

You've now dealt with characters, objects, styles, continuity, revisions, formats, quality assurance, and publication requirements.

So let's ask a different question:

> ## **Would you confidently publish it?**

A professional visual asset doesn't just need to look good.

It needs to be:

**Good · Correct · Consistent · Controllable · Editable · Reviewable**
